## Post 1

**Post Date:** 2026-06-18 (approx., "1d" relative timestamp at time of research)

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_reddit-marketing-activity-7473029421369597953-bLMw](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_reddit-marketing-activity-7473029421369597953-bLMw)

**Summary:**  
Ross argues that Google's growing visibility of Reddit content is not the result of favoritism toward the platform, but rather a response to user behavior. For years, searchers have intentionally added "reddit" to their searches because they wanted authentic experiences and real-world opinions instead of heavily optimized marketing content. He notes that after Google's May 2026 core update, Reddit's number-one rankings reportedly increased by 54%, signaling Google's increasing emphasis on experience-driven content. Ross frames Reddit as more than a social platform: it functions simultaneously as a customer research tool, reputation-monitoring channel, and content strategy resource. He encourages marketers to examine how real users discuss their products, competitors, pricing, onboarding, and implementation experiences on Reddit, warning that disconnects between marketing claims and customer experiences are often visible there long before companies realize it.

**Key Takeaways:**

- User behavior, not platform favoritism, is driving Reddit's growing search visibility
- Buyers increasingly seek experience-based insights alongside traditional expert content
- Reddit can serve as a valuable source of customer research, competitive intelligence, and reputation monitoring
- Comparing brand messaging with real customer discussions can reveal trust gaps and positioning weaknesses
- Ross consistently emphasizes distribution channels where authentic audience conversations already exist rather than relying solely on company-controlled content

## Post 2

**Post Date:** 2026-06-16 (approx., "3d" relative timestamp at time of research)

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_marketing-activity-7472634098751279104-oUKJ](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_marketing-activity-7472634098751279104-oUKJ)

**Summary:**  
Ross argues that one of the biggest shifts happening in marketing is the transition from a traffic-driven mindset to an influence-driven mindset. Historically, marketers focused heavily on generating clicks, website visits, and pageviews. Ross suggests that in an AI-powered search environment, visibility increasingly comes from being cited, referenced, and trusted across platforms and communities rather than simply earning direct traffic. He highlights the growing importance of becoming a recognized source of expertise that influences conversations within ecosystems marketers do not directly control. The post promotes an upcoming discussion on how this shift affects SEO, content strategy, distribution, and community-building, emphasizing practical applications rather than theoretical trend analysis.

**Key Takeaways:**

- Marketing success is increasingly measured by influence and authority rather than raw traffic volume
- Being cited and referenced across multiple platforms can create stronger long-term visibility than chasing clicks alone
- Communities, trusted voices, and third-party references are becoming more important in modern content distribution
- AI-driven search environments reward brands that establish credibility and expertise across the broader ecosystem
- Ross consistently emphasizes distribution and influence as strategic assets rather than treating content creation as the end goal

## Post 3

**Post Date:** 2026-06-16 (approx., "3d" relative timestamp at time of research)

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_marketing-contentmarketing-semrushpartner-activity-7472290587103170560-X0ka](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rosssimmonds_marketing-contentmarketing-semrushpartner-activity-7472290587103170560-X0ka)

**Summary:**  
Ross announces a new certification program focused on AI visibility, developed alongside several recognized SEO and content marketing practitioners. The post centers on a question that has become increasingly important in modern search: if buyers are turning to AI systems for recommendations and research, why do so many brands remain invisible within AI-generated responses? Drawing on nearly two years of research, audits of hundreds of brands, and extensive data analysis, Ross argues that most organizations are missing the foundational elements required for AI visibility. He introduces a framework built around four core pillars—Discoverability, Clarity, Authority, and Trust—which together determine whether AI systems can find, understand, recommend, and confidently reference a brand. The post reflects Ross's broader focus on adapting content and distribution strategies to emerging AI-driven discovery channels.

**Key Takeaways:**

- AI visibility is emerging as a distinct marketing discipline alongside traditional SEO
- Many brands remain absent from AI-generated recommendations because they lack foundational discoverability and positioning signals
- Effective AI visibility requires more than content production; it depends on clarity, authority, and trust signals across the web
- Research-backed frameworks and structured audits can help organizations identify gaps in how AI systems interpret their brands
- Ross consistently focuses on helping marketers adapt distribution strategies to changes in how information is discovered and consumed
