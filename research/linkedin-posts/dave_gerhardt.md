## Post 1

**Post Date:** 2026-06-18 (approx., "1d" relative timestamp at time of research)

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davegerhardt_do-not-come-to-our-annual-event-for-exit-activity-7473327989108236289-CcIa](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davegerhardt_do-not-come-to-our-annual-event-for-exit-activity-7473327989108236289-CcIa)

**Summary:**  
Dave promotes Exit Five's annual B2B marketing event using a reverse-psychology approach, opening with "Do not come" and then listing the specific reasons marketers actually should attend. Rather than focusing on celebrity speakers or generic conference benefits, he emphasizes practical value: learning from real practitioners running real campaigns, building meaningful peer relationships, and joining a community known for authenticity and collaboration. He also highlights the event experience itself, including venue quality, networking opportunities, and strong Net Promoter Scores from previous years. The post concludes with a humorous acknowledgment that the entire structure is an intentional sales technique inspired by classic reverse-psychology outreach.

**Key Takeaways:**

- Reverse-psychology hooks can make promotional content feel more engaging and less sales-heavy than traditional event marketing
- Emphasizing community, relationships, and peer learning can be more compelling than focusing solely on speakers or presentations
- Specific proof points (such as NPS scores and ticket availability) add credibility and urgency to promotional content
- In an era of AI-generated content, positioning human relationships and practitioner knowledge as differentiators is a powerful marketing angle
- Self-awareness and humor can make promotional posts feel more authentic and relatable

## Post 2

**Post Date:** 2026-06-12 (approx., "1w" relative timestamp at time of research)

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davegerhardt_we-polled-540-b2b-marketers-on-how-they-feel-activity-7470420490621464576-MWC](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davegerhardt_we-polled-540-b2b-marketers-on-how-they-feel-activity-7470420490621464576-MWC_)_

**Summary:**  
Dave shares insights from a survey of 540 B2B marketers about their attitudes toward AI. While he acknowledges that the poll is informal and not scientifically rigorous, he uses it to identify broader industry trends. The findings suggest that AI adoption is already widespread among marketers, with most respondents using AI tools daily or weekly. Contrary to common narratives about AI replacing jobs, respondents expressed greater concern about low-quality, AI-generated content flooding marketing channels than about losing their own roles. Dave also highlights that senior marketing leaders, particularly CMOs and VPs, are among the most enthusiastic adopters of AI, interpreting this as evidence that AI is shifting leadership attention away from operational management and back toward strategic judgment, creativity, and taste. The post concludes with a call-to-action directing readers to his newsletter for the complete survey results.

**Key Takeaways:**

- Original research, even when informal, can generate highly engaging content when it provides useful directional insights for a target audience
- B2B marketers appear more concerned about content quality and market saturation than direct job displacement from AI
- Senior marketing leaders are often among the fastest adopters of emerging technologies because they view them as strategic advantages rather than threats
- Presenting survey findings as observations rather than absolute truths increases credibility and transparency
- Sharing selected insights publicly while reserving the full analysis for a newsletter is an effective content-to-subscriber acquisition strategy

## Post 3

**Post Date:** 2026-06-05 (approx., "2w" relative timestamp at time of research)

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davegerhardt_i-love-writing-about-abm-and-our-audience-activity-7468617621387636736-Z35m](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/davegerhardt_i-love-writing-about-abm-and-our-audience-activity-7468617621387636736-Z35m)

**Summary:**  
Dave shares key lessons from a conversation with Casey Patterson, Director of ABM at Snowflake, about what makes account-based marketing (ABM) effective in modern B2B organizations. He argues that ABM is unique because it naturally aligns marketing and sales around a shared objective rather than encouraging attribution debates. Drawing from Patterson's experience, he highlights several principles: building target account lists based on sales capacity rather than total market size, concentrating marketing efforts into focused "swarm" campaigns around individual accounts, prioritizing sales alignment over expensive marketing technology, and preparing for a future where AI enables personalized account engagement at scale. The post blends practical advice, strategic thinking, and examples from a recognized ABM practitioner.

**Key Takeaways:**

- Successful ABM programs begin with sales capacity and operational constraints, not theoretical market size
- Focused, time-bound campaigns around specific accounts often outperform continuous, always-on marketing efforts
- Strong sales and marketing alignment is more important than expensive software or large technology stacks
- Personalized account engagement is becoming increasingly scalable through AI and automation
- Mentorship and professional networking can create long-term career opportunities, as demonstrated through Casey Patterson's relationship with a future manager
