## Post 1

**Post Date:** 2026-06-18 (approx., "1d" relative timestamp at time of research)

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_jay-acunzo-has-talked-for-a-long-time-about-activity-7473133780807995392-hQW0](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_jay-acunzo-has-talked-for-a-long-time-about-activity-7473133780807995392-hQW0)

**Summary:**  
Amanda builds on Jay Acunzo's idea of "resonance over reach," agreeing that marketers often become overly focused on maximizing impressions at the expense of creating memorable work. However, she adds an important nuance: marketers cannot completely ignore reach because distribution remains essential for business growth. Instead, she argues for a sequence where resonance comes first and reach comes second. In her view, content should initially be specific, meaningful, and memorable to a particular audience before being scaled to larger audiences. She uses this idea to promote an episode of her Zero Click Marketing podcast, where she and Jay discuss topics such as public speaking as a zero-click channel, persuasive communication, and creating memorable marketing.

**Key Takeaways:**

- Content that reaches many people but fails to create meaningful impact is ultimately less valuable than content that deeply resonates with a smaller audience
- Marketers should prioritize creating memorable, audience-specific content before focusing on distribution and scale
- Reach and resonance are not opposing goals; effective marketing often requires achieving resonance first and then expanding reach
- Public speaking can function as a "zero-click" marketing channel because it builds trust and awareness without requiring immediate clicks or conversions
- Strong marketing increasingly depends on the ability to communicate and defend ideas effectively, not just generate visibility

## Post 2

**Post Date:** 2026-06-17 (approx., "2d" relative timestamp at time of research)

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_how-would-you-start-a-marketing-strategy-activity-7472780094164467712-l9dM](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_how-would-you-start-a-marketing-strategy-activity-7472780094164467712-l9dM)

**Summary:**  
Amanda answers a webinar question about how to build a marketing strategy from scratch. Rather than starting with channels or tactics, she argues that marketers should first understand the company's underlying growth model. She outlines three common growth motions: discovery-led businesses that rely on search and awareness, product-led businesses that grow through user adoption and sharing, and sales-led businesses that depend on relationship-building and long buying cycles. Each growth model requires different marketing priorities. After identifying the growth mechanism, she recommends finding the fastest way to demonstrate marketing's value by improving the most important part of that growth process. While long-term brand building and strategic initiatives remain important, she believes marketers must first earn credibility internally by supporting the company's primary growth engine.

**Key Takeaways:**

- Effective marketing strategy begins with understanding how the business is designed to grow, not by choosing channels or tactics first
- Different growth models require different content priorities:
  - Discovery-led businesses benefit from SEO, educational content, and comparison pages
  - Product-led businesses benefit from onboarding content, templates, lifecycle emails, and activation-focused resources
  - Sales-led businesses benefit from sales enablement assets such as buyer guides and follow-up sequences
- Marketing teams should focus on the shortest path to proving business impact before expanding into larger strategic initiatives
- Building internal trust often comes from solving immediate business problems rather than launching ambitious long-term programs
- Aligning marketing activities with the company's growth motion creates clearer priorities and stronger business outcomes

## Post 3

**Post Date:** 2026-06-12 (approx., "1w" relative timestamp at time of research)

**Source:** [https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_i-dont-believe-in-launch-days-sure-a-activity-7468684562353729537-JSLc](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amandanat_i-dont-believe-in-launch-days-sure-a-activity-7468684562353729537-JSLc)

**Summary:**  
Amanda challenges the common idea of a product launch being a single event or announcement. Instead, she argues that successful launches are gradual rollouts that unfold over weeks or months. She outlines a four-stage process: first engaging highly interested customers who specifically requested the feature, then expanding to the broader customer base, followed by prospects and free users, and finally making a public announcement. This sequence allows companies to accumulate testimonials, use cases, screenshots, and social proof before reaching a larger audience. By the time the public launch occurs, the company can demonstrate real-world validation rather than simply announcing something new. Amanda emphasizes that the strongest launches feel like a rollout supported by customer evidence, not a one-day marketing event.

**Key Takeaways:**

- Product launches should be viewed as multi-stage rollouts rather than single-day announcements
- Early adopters and existing customers provide valuable social proof that strengthens later marketing efforts
- Gathering testimonials, use cases, and customer feedback before a public launch increases credibility
- Expanding launch communications in stages helps match messaging to audience readiness and awareness
- Public announcements become more persuasive when supported by real examples of customer success
- Strong launches focus on proof and adoption rather than simply generating attention on launch day
