## Post 1

**Post Date:** 2026-06-05 (approx., "2w" relative timestamp at time of research)
**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/goel-aman_can-a-startup-actually-raise-capital-from-activity-7467512092544262145-0U9_

**Summary:**
Aman shares his framework for startup fundraising, grounded in his own experience raising ₹100 crore for GreyLabs AI within a year across two rounds. He breaks the process down to three core questions VCs are really evaluating: Team (do the founders have a genuine, hard-to-copy unfair advantage for this specific opportunity, i.e. founder-market fit), TAM (is the market large enough, or rapidly compounding enough, to produce a venture-scale outcome), and Timing (why is now the right moment — not too early, not too late — often tied to a shift in technology, regulation, or customer behavior). He argues that once these three are genuinely strong, supporting elements like the pitch deck and metrics are just packaging, and ends by asking his audience what they'd add as a fourth factor.

**Key Takeaways:**
- Sharing a clear, reusable framework (Team / TAM / Timing) backed by personal results (₹100 crore raised) is a strong way to build credibility while giving genuinely useful, repeatable advice
- "Founder-market fit" (why you specifically are positioned to win) is emphasized as more important to investors than the idea itself
- Ending with an open question ("What would you add as a fourth T?") is a deliberate, simple tactic to drive engagement and comments

## Post 2

**Post Date:** 2026-05-19 (approx., "1mo" relative timestamp at time of research)
**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/goel-aman_most-young-founders-are-told-to-follow-your-activity-7461711065345818625-BJDo

**Summary:**
Aman challenges the common advice to "follow your passion," arguing that most people in their early 20s simply haven't lived enough to know what their passion actually is. He shares his own story: as a computer science graduate, he didn't start out passionate about "AI for financial services" — he simply spotted a real opportunity, landing a ₹75 lakh contract with SBI while still in college, which led to further deals with SBI Life, SBI Mutual Fund, and ICICI Bank. That first venture scaled past $1 million in revenue and was acquired in 2021. Nine years later, what began as opportunity-chasing has become genuine passion for solving problems in financial inclusion and credit access. His core argument: passion is often a byproduct of sustained effort and exposure, not a prerequisite for starting.

**Key Takeaways:**
- Contrarian takes on common career advice ("follow your passion" is wrong) are an effective hook, especially when backed by a credible personal track record
- Concrete numbers and named clients (₹75 lakh SBI contract, $1M+ revenue, 2021 acquisition) lend specificity and credibility that vague founder advice often lacks
- Reframing personal experience into a broader, generalizable lesson ("passion is the reward for showing up, not the starting point") gives a personal story wider relevance to the audience

## Post 3

**Post Date:** 2026-05-19 (approx., "1mo" relative timestamp at time of research)
**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/goel-aman_ten-years-ago-i-landed-in-san-francisco-activity-7458797061887569920-thTn

**Summary:**
Aman reflects on a 10-year arc of his career, starting with a software engineering internship at Rubrik in Palo Alto as a 20-year-old IIT Bombay student in 2016. He credits that experience — and a strong mentor — with giving him clarity that he wanted to return to India and build his own company rather than stay in the Bay Area. That decision led him to spend his final year of college focused on entrepreneurship and business-building rather than just coursework, eventually co-founding Cogno AI (bootstrapped past $1M in revenue, later acquired), and now leading GreyLabs AI, which has raised close to ₹100 crore from Z47 and Elevation Capital, grown to 85+ employees, and serves 75+ BFSI accounts with 3x year-over-year growth. He closes by encouraging current interns to pay attention to what excites them, framing that signal as more valuable than the stipend itself.

**Key Takeaways:**
- Long-arc "origin story" posts that connect an early formative experience to present-day outcomes are an effective way to build narrative credibility over time, not just in a single moment
- Concrete, verifiable business metrics (funding amount, investors, team size, client count, growth rate) reinforce credibility within a personal storytelling format
- Encouraging early-career readers (interns) to act on the post's lesson broadens its relevance beyond just fellow founders
