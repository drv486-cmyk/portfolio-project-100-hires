**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/paraschopra_midjourney-launching-an-ultrasound-scanner-activity-7473230669469360129-7dWt

**Summary:**
Paras comments on Midjourney's launch of a full-body ultrasound scanner — a major pivot into hardware and healthcare for a company best known for AI image generation. He frames this as a clear example of the kind of bold, unconventional bet that bootstrapped companies can make but VC-backed companies typically can't, since founders without external investors retain full ownership and decision-making freedom. He's careful to flag that the bet's success is unproven ("only time will tell"), but argues this kind of "devil-may-care" risk-taking requires the ownership structure that bootstrapped founders have.

**Key Takeaways:**
- This is commentary on a genuine, very recent news event (Midjourney announced its medical division and ultrasound scanner on June 18, 2026) — verified accurate, not fabricated or exaggerated
- Core thesis: ownership structure (bootstrapped vs. VC-backed) directly shapes founders' willingness to make unconventional, high-risk strategic bets
- Avoids overclaiming the outcome — explicitly flags that success is unknown, which adds credibility
- Short, sharp commentary on a single piece of breaking industry news is an efficient content format that relies on timely judgment rather than original research

## Post 3

**Post Date:** 2026-06-14 (approx., "5d" relative timestamp at time of research)
**Source:** https://www.linkedin.com/posts/paraschopra_i-see-a-lot-of-enthusiasm-about-building-activity-7471791226006962177-yi9j

**Summary:**
Paras pushes back on enthusiasm around India building "sovereign" frontier AI models, arguing that building a frontier-class model is fundamentally a compute and funding problem, not just a talent or ambition problem. He cites a rough estimate that India has access to roughly 50–100K H100-equivalent GPUs, compared to frontier labs with access to roughly a million each, and challenges his audience to name an Indian company with funding/compute close to Chinese or American AI labs. He argues that without addressing this gap, India risks repeating a familiar pattern of ambitious announcements followed by models that lag behind the frontier. He offers two paths forward: serious investment in basic R&D to find a new training paradigm, or a large-scale government commitment of compute resources.

**Key Takeaways:**
- Willingness to challenge popular sentiment with hard numbers and structural reasoning builds credibility as a thoughtful operator, not just a cheerleader
- Grounding strategic arguments in specific, checkable figures makes commentary more substantive than vague opinion
- Ending with concrete alternative paths forward, rather than just criticism, makes the post constructive rather than purely contrarian
